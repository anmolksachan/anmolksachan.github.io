Time Traveling for Bugs: How The Time Machine v3.0 Uncovered an XSS on REDACTED.com [Part 1]

Anmol K Sachan · August 4, 2025 · 3 min read
Share: Tweet Share View on Medium ↗

Hey everyone! As someone who loves digging into web applications, whether for bug bounties or just pure curiosity, I’m always on the lookout for tools that make the recon phase smoother. We all know the Wayback Machine is a goldmine of historical data, but sifting through it manually can be a nightmare. That’s where tools like TheTimeMachine come in, and let me tell you, it’s a game-changer.

Recently, I decided to take TheTimeMachine for a spin, specifically focusing on finding Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) vulnerabilities. And guess what? It delivered! I wanted to share the quick and dirty details of how it went down.

The Setup: Fetching the Past

First things first, you need to arm TheTimeMachine with historical URLs. The tool is an OSINT framework that leverages the Wayback Machine, so the initial step is to pull all those archived links for your target. For this particular test, I pointed it at REDACTED.com.

The command is pretty straightforward:

python3 thetimemachine.py REDACTED.com --fetch

Watching it go was cool. The console continued to churn through URLs, in batches of 5,000. It felt like I was pulling data from different temporal dimensions! In total, it retrieved a whopping 595,507 URLs for REDACTED.com and saved them neatly into a text file. Talk about a treasure trove of endpoints!

Retrieved 595507 URLs and saved to content/REDACTED.com/REDACTED.com_URLs.txt

The Hunt: Unleashing the XSS Attack Mode

With all those URLs in hand, it was time for the fun part: the attack mode. The Time Machine isn’t just a scraper; it’s designed to scan for vulnerable endpoints using predefined patterns and signatures. For XSS, it comes with a list of common payloads.

I specifically targeted XSS with the --attack xss flag:

python3 thetimemachine.py REDACTED.com --attack xss

The tool got right to work, scanning through the massive list of URLs. The magic happens when it finds a parameter that looks like it might be vulnerable. In this case, it was the USERNAME parameter that caught its eye.

And then, boom! The flagged URLs started popping up:

Preparing the time machine portal for voyager....\
[~] Scanning content/REDACTED.com/REDACTED.com_URLs.txt for XSS patterns...[+] Flagged: https://online.networks.REDACTED.com/siteminderagent/forms/smpwservices.fcc?USERNAME=test&SMAUTHREASON=7
[+] Flagged: https://online.networks.REDACTED.com/siteminderagent/forms/smpwservices.fcc?USERNAME=[REDACTED]&SMAUTHREASON=7
[+] Flagged: https://online.networks.REDACTED.com/siteminderagent/forms/smpwservices.fcc?USERNAME=[REDACTED]&SMAUTHREASON=7
[!] 2 URLs matched for XSS - saved to content/REDACTED.com/REDACTED.com_xss.txt
 Saved 2 URLs to content/REDACTED.com/REDACTED.com_xss.txt
[+] Saved 2 possible XSS links to content/REDACTED.com/REDACTED.com_xss.txt

The tool successfully flagged two URLs, both pointing to online.networks.REDACTED.com and both showing potential XSS in the USERNAME parameter. The payloads used were classic <img> tags with onerror events, designed to trigger a confirm box. This is a clear indicator of a reflected XSS vulnerability where user input isn't properly sanitized before being rendered back on the page.

The Takeaway

Finding XSS, especially in older or forgotten parts of a web application, can be incredibly valuable. These archived endpoints might not be actively maintained or monitored, making them prime targets for overlooked vulnerabilities. The Time Machine shines here by automating the process of sifting through years of web history.

It’s not just about XSS either. The tool has modes for SQLi, LFI, open redirects, and even specific checks for WordPress and JIRA misconfigurations. Plus, its ability to extract GET parameters for fuzzing and detect JWTs makes it a comprehensive recon suite.

If you’re into bug bounties or just want to level up your OSINT game, I highly recommend checking out TheTimeMachine. It’s built by folks who clearly understand the pain points of recon, and it shows in its effectiveness. Happy hunting!
